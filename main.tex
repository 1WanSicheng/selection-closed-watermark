\begin{abstract}
Distortion-free language-model watermarks guarantee that a \emph{single}
watermarked generation has the same marginal law as unwatermarked text.
Inference-time alignment and test-time scaling, however, generate multiple
candidates and select among them --- by a reward model, verifier, reranker,
rejection rule, or self-consistency vote. We show that the marginal guarantee
is not preserved by such selection. In deterministic context-hashed schemes,
reusing one key-derived randomness stream across candidates makes all $N$
candidates identical, eliminating the entire Best-of-$N$ alignment gain; more
generally, candidate dependence can bias the post-selection output even when
every candidate marginal is exactly correct. We introduce
\emph{$N$-selection-closed} distortion-freeness, which requires the candidate
tuple to match $N$ independent unwatermarked samples, and prove that joint
preservation is \emph{necessary and sufficient} for closure under every
key-independent selector --- marginal preservation alone provides no control
over the post-selection distribution. The repair is a minimal, scheme-agnostic
lift based on candidate-specific subkeys: independently sampled keys yield
exact joint closure, and PRF-derived subkeys yield computational joint closure
under a single master key. Existing detectors lift through a union test with an
$O(\sqrt{\log N})$ threshold penalty and $N$-fold parallel evaluation. On
LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, shared-key watermarking produces $1/16$ unique
candidates and forfeits the full Best-of-$16$ reward gain; candidate-separated
subkeys restore $16/16$ unique candidates, recover the full available gain
(reward $0.0861$ vs.\ unwatermarked Best-of-$N$ $0.0848$) with each candidate's
marginal law unchanged, and retain strong post-selection detection
($\approx0.3$ z-score cost). The same failure and repair reproduce across a
second distortion-free decoder family and a family of inference-time
selectors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Distortion-free watermarking and inference-time selection address different
goals. The former modifies generation while preserving its single-sample
distribution \citep{aaronson2023, kuditipudi2023robust, zhao2024permute}; the
latter improves alignment or reasoning by generating multiple candidates and
selecting among them, as in Best-of-$N$ reward selection. Recent work has
begun to combine the two --- for example, using Best-of-$N$ to recover the
alignment quality degraded by watermarking \citep{verma2025watermarking} ---
but their distributional compatibility is not understood.

A distortion-free guarantee is typically \emph{marginal}: one watermarked
output, averaged over the secret key, follows the unwatermarked distribution.
Best-of-$N$, however, is a \emph{joint} procedure: its behavior depends not
only on the marginal law of each candidate but also on their dependence. This
raises a basic compositional question:
\begin{center}
\emph{Does marginal distortion-freeness suffice once a selector observes
multiple watermarked candidates?}
\end{center}
We show that it does not. Under a shared deterministic key stream, all
candidates can collapse to a single output, eliminating the entire
Best-of-$N$ gain; more generally, exact candidate marginals can coexist with
a correlated joint law that changes the selected-output distribution. A
minimal, scheme-agnostic change --- candidate-specific keys --- restores the
guarantee at the selection level.

\textbf{The collapse.} In context-hashed deterministic schemes (Gumbel/Aaronson-style,
KGW derivatives, SynthID-Text), the per-step noise is a pseudorandom function of the
key and the local context. All $N$ BoN candidates for a prompt start from the same
context, so they read the same noise and make the same argmax choice --- and identical
prefixes are a fixed point of the dynamics, so the candidates remain identical
forever: generation is a deterministic function of (key, prompt), and no step ever
creates a first divergence. On LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct we observe exactly $1/16$
unique candidates per prompt --- Figure~\ref{fig:transcript} shows a verbatim
instance. Selection then has nothing to select from: every
selection head returns the single-sample reward ($0.0693$), forfeiting the entire
BoN gain (unwatermarked BoN reaches $0.0848$ at $N{=}16$). Complete collapse is
only the endpoint of the problem: partial candidate correlation also reduces
effective test-time compute --- the selector receives fewer independent
opportunities to find a high-reward output --- and biases the post-selection
distribution even when every candidate marginal is exact, with the bias
\emph{growing} in $N$ (Figure~\ref{fig:toy}; in the comonotone worst case ---
realized by the shared-key instantiation --- the shift approaches
TV $=\tfrac12$, Example~\ref{ex:comonotone}). The de facto workaround ---
softening the sampler with fresh randomness \citep{verma2025watermarking} ---
restores diversity but breaks the exact marginal certification it was meant to
protect, and we show it worsens the reward--detection--distribution trade-off
relative to our construction.

\textbf{Why the composition matters.} The importance of the question is
structural, not a claim about how widely any one pipeline is used. First, the
Best-of-$N$ gain is a property of the \emph{joint} candidate law: for
equicorrelated rewards the expected maximum grows as
$\mu+\sigma\sqrt{1-\rho}\,m_N$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:attenuation}), so a watermark
that changes candidate dependence changes the test-time scaling law itself,
even when every marginal is exact. Second, the claim ``does not change the
generation distribution'' becomes ambiguous under selection: it may mean that
each \emph{candidate} is unchanged, that the candidate \emph{pool} is
unchanged, or that the \emph{selected output} is unchanged. Marginal
distortion-freeness delivers only the first; this paper delimits which of the
three a given scheme actually provides. Third, the failure grows with
inference-time compute: under a shared key additional candidates add nothing,
and under partial correlation the gap to independent Best-of-$N$ widens with
$N$. Watermarking can thus change how model performance \emph{scales} with
inference-time compute.

\textbf{Not merely a diversity or seeding issue.} It is well known that
deterministic decoding with reused randomness reduces candidate diversity
\citep{verma2025watermarking}, and assigning different randomness to
different candidates is an operationally natural fix. Neither observation
answers the underlying question: \emph{what property must a watermarked
candidate pool satisfy so that an arbitrary inference-time selector behaves
as it would on unwatermarked samples?} The issue persists beyond complete
stream reuse --- any dependence among candidates can alter the output of a
reward-based selector while leaving every marginal unchanged --- and
distinctness of candidates alone does not give a correct selected-output
distribution. Our contribution is to identify and characterize the required
joint guarantee; candidate-specific domain separation is then the minimal
construction that realizes it. The tension is structural: the certification
constrains only the first moment, over keys, of the key-conditional output
law; a pool of $N$ candidates generated under one key is governed by its
higher moments; and detection requires exactly the second-moment deviation
that selection exposes --- so no detectable watermark can be
selection-neutral under a shared key, stochastic or deterministic
(Proposition~\ref{prop:moments}, sharpening to the selection setting the
key-collision impossibility of \citealp{keycollision2024}).

\textbf{Formalization.} We define \emph{$N$-selection-closed} distortion-freeness:
the joint distribution of the $N$ candidates (and hence of the output of \emph{any}
selection operator applied to them) matches that of $N$ i.i.d.\ unwatermarked samples.
Single-sample (marginal) distortion-freeness does not imply it; joint
preservation is exactly the right condition --- necessary and sufficient
(Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}). The property sits strictly between the marginal guarantee and cryptographic
undetectability \citep{christ2023undetectable}, which implies it
computationally but has no practical instantiation
(Proposition~\ref{prop:hierarchy}).

\textbf{Repair.} We emphasize what this paper is \emph{not}: it proposes no
new watermarking algorithm, and the mechanism we analyze is deliberately
unoriginal --- domain separation is textbook cryptographic hygiene. The
contribution is the missing selection-level property and the proof that this
standard mechanism is a \emph{complete and minimal} repair. The jointly-coupled
transform $\mathrm{JC}_N$: given any keyed sampler $W$ whose per-step
randomness is a pseudorandom function of its key, candidate $i$ runs $W$
unmodified under $\mathrm{SubKey}_i=\mathrm{PRF}(\mathrm{MasterKey},\,i)$.
The $N$ candidates become i.i.d.\ copies of $W$'s single-sample law ---
exactly, if the $N$ keys are sampled and stored independently;
computationally, under the compact master key --- so whatever marginal
guarantee $W$ has is inherited \emph{jointly}: marginally distortion-free $W$
becomes $N$-selection-closed at the corresponding tier
(Theorems~\ref{thm:exactlift} and~\ref{thm:lifting}), for every selection
operator.
Detection lifts the same way: the \emph{union wrapper} runs $W$'s own detector
under each subkey (only $N$, or an upper bound, need be known --- not the
selected index), paying a once-per-sequence threshold penalty of
$\sqrt{2\ln N}$ --- empirically $0.3$ z-score at $N{=}16$ against a signal of
$z\approx7.7$ --- and recovering the winning candidate's index as a free audit
log. Figure~\ref{fig:detectors} verifies that the union detector matches the
correct-subkey oracle at every $N$ and entropy level.

\textbf{Results.} On LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct with HH-RLHF prompts and an ArmoRM reward:
(i) strict shared-key BoN returns identical candidates and zero BoN gain;
(ii) subkeys restore $16/16$ unique candidates and full BoN reward
($0.0861$ vs.\ the unwatermarked Best-of-$N$ reference $0.0848$), with
single-sample reward matching
the unwatermarked model ($0.0698$ vs.\ $0.0703$; the candidate marginal is
unchanged);
(iii) union detection retains $z=7.71$ vs.\ $8.00$ for the shared key;
(iv) relative to the softened workaround, candidate separation attains equal
or better reward ($0.0861$ vs.\ $0.0852$) and stronger post-BoN detection
($z$ $6.50$ vs.\ $5.62$) while preserving the exact marginal certification
that softening gives up.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{A failure of marginal distortion-freeness under test-time
        selection.} Single-sample distortion-freeness does not imply
        correctness after Best-of-$N$, reward reranking, rejection sampling,
        or other selection rules; under shared deterministic watermark
        randomness all candidates collapse to one sample, eliminating the
        entire test-time alignment gain (\S\ref{sec:collapse}).
  \item \emph{The correct joint guarantee.} We define $N$-selection-closed
        distortion-freeness and prove that preservation of the candidate joint
        law is \emph{necessary and sufficient} for closure under every
        key-independent selector; marginal preservation alone gives no control
        over the selected output. Candidate correlation $\rho$ attenuates the
        test-time scaling gain by exactly $\sqrt{1-\rho}$. Moreover,
        under a shared key, joint closure at any $N\ge2$ forces the
        watermark to carry no distributional signal at all --- the
        selection-level counterpart of the key-collision impossibility of
        \citet{keycollision2024} --- and we quantify the entire middle
        range: one key-covariance operator carries detectability, the
        scaling attenuation, and a selector-uniform drift bound
        (\S\ref{sec:theory}).
  \item \emph{A minimal selection-compatible lift.} Independently keyed
        candidates give exact joint closure; candidate-specific subkeys
        derived from one master key give computational joint closure under PRF
        security. The construction applies to existing keyed decoders without
        modifying their token-level sampling rule (\S\ref{sec:method}).
  \item \emph{Detection after candidate separation.} The base detector lifts
        through a union test over candidate subkeys: an $O(\sqrt{\log N})$
        statistical threshold penalty --- order-optimal for index-blind
        detection --- plus $N$ parallel base-detector evaluations. The null
        law of the union statistic is exact at every text length, and the
        penalty, measured per token, vanishes as $O(T^{-1/2})$ with text
        length $T$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:vanish}, \S\ref{sec:detection}).
  \item \emph{Validation across selectors and watermark families.} Across
        hard/soft/Exp Best-of-$N$, rejection sampling, filter-and-sample, and
        stress selectors, candidate separation restores the reward and
        diversity behavior of independent inference while retaining strong
        detection; the same pattern holds for a second deterministic
        distortion-free decoder family, and a biased-logit family (KGW)
        collapses exactly when derandomized and is repaired with its
        detector unchanged (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Distortion-free watermarking.}
Gumbel/exponential schemes \citep{aaronson2023}, inverse-transform and robust
variants \citep{kuditipudi2023robust}, permute-and-flip
\citep{zhao2024permute}, and semantic-level schemes \citep{pmark2025} certify
a single-sample (or key-averaged) distributional guarantee: one watermarked
output, averaged over the key, follows the unwatermarked law. SynthID-Text
\citep{dathathri2024scalable} deploys the context-hash family at scale with
repeated-context masking, which addresses intra-sequence reuse, not
cross-candidate correlation. At the strong end of the guarantee spectrum,
\citet{christ2023undetectable} construct computationally \emph{undetectable}
watermarks; undetectability implies selection-closure computationally but has
no practical instantiation, whereas the property we isolate is strictly
weaker and is reached by existing decoders through a key-management change
(Proposition~\ref{prop:hierarchy}).

\textbf{Multi-candidate watermarking and alignment.}
A small number of works combine watermarked generation with multiple
candidates. Alignment Resampling \citep{verma2025watermarking} uses
reward-based Best-of-$N$ to recover the alignment quality lost to
watermarking, softening the sampler with fresh randomness and thereby giving
up the exact marginal guarantee. WaterMax \citep{giboulot2024watermax} and
WaterSearch \citep{watersearch2025} make selection part of the watermark
itself, choosing among drafts by watermark score (WaterSearch also uses
per-candidate seeds as a mechanism, without a distributional analysis of the
resulting candidate pool). \citet{keycollision2024} show that key collisions
across generations induce correlations that break distortion-freeness of the
generated \emph{text}: they formalize per-generation (``weakly'') versus
joint-across-generations (``strongly'') distortion-freeness and prove the
strong form unattainable under key collision --- the text-distribution
antecedent of our Proposition~\ref{prop:moments}(ii). Their object is the
corpus-level distribution over repeated queries with \emph{different}
prompts, where collisions are partial and the bias is spread across the
corpus, and their remedy mitigates it within the shared key; no selection
operator appears. Our setting is the $N$-candidate pool for a \emph{single}
prompt --- the extreme collision, at every step --- where an external
selector converts the joint correlation into first-order bias of the one
\emph{released} output; we characterize this selection-facing consequence,
quantify the drift (Proposition~\ref{prop:drift}), and remove the collision
across candidates by key separation. Multi-bit watermarking \citep{fernandez2023three} runs detection
under every message key and takes the best p-value --- the technical
precedent for our union detector. Prior work thus either uses selection to
improve watermark strength, or uses selection to recover quality under a
watermarked sampler. We instead study whether the distortion-free
\emph{guarantee itself} is preserved when an external alignment or
inference-time selector acts on multiple watermarked candidates, and which
joint property makes it so. The random-offset construction of
\citet{kuditipudi2023robust} decorrelates candidates approximately, at the
cost of moving to alignment-based (edit-distance) detection; candidate
separation keeps the strong context-hash detectors.

% Mechanism: determinism + identical initial condition -> identical trajectories.
\subsection{Setup}
Let $\pi$ denote the language model, $x$ a prompt, and consider $N$ candidate
generations $Y_1,\dots,Y_N$ produced by a watermarked decoder with key $k$.
A \emph{selection operator} $S$ (e.g., $\arg\max$ of a reward model $r$) returns
$Y_{S}$. In context-hash schemes, the per-step noise at position $t$ is
$g_t = \mathrm{PRF}(k, \mathrm{ctx}_t)$ where $\mathrm{ctx}_t$ is the local
$h$-gram context, and the token is chosen by a deterministic rule (argmax) given
logits and noise.

\subsection{A thirty-second failure}\label{sec:coin}
Let each candidate be a fair coin flip and let the selector prefer heads. With
i.i.d.\ candidates, Best-of-$N$ returns heads with probability $1-2^{-N}$. Now
couple the candidates into $N$ copies of a \emph{single} flip: every copy is
still a perfectly fair coin --- the certified, marginal guarantee is intact ---
yet the selector returns heads with probability $\tfrac12$. The
post-selection output laws differ by $\mathrm{TV}=\tfrac12-2^{-N}\to\tfrac12$: marginal
correctness survives, the selection-level guarantee does not, and the gap
grows with
the very compute ($N$) that selection adds. The strict shared key realizes
exactly this coupling (Figure~\ref{fig:transcript});
Example~\ref{ex:comonotone} gives the general statement.

\subsection{Identical candidates: an induction}
All candidates share the prompt, hence the initial context, hence the initial noise
and the initial token; by induction every subsequent context, noise, and token
coincide. Identical prefixes are a fixed point of the dynamics and the process
starts in it: generation is a deterministic function of $(k,x)$, so the $N$
candidates are $N$ copies. There is no source of a first divergence.
Empirically (\S\ref{sec:experiments}): $1.0/16$ unique candidates per prompt under
the strict shared key; the softened variant diverges stochastically but retains a
$2.2\times$ longer shared prefix than unwatermarked sampling
(Appendix~\ref{app:exp}).

\subsection{Partial correlation and scaling attenuation}\label{sec:toy}
Marginal correctness with joint correlation suffices to bias any selector.
We construct candidates with exact marginals and tunable correlation $\rho$
(Gaussian copula), select by noisy reward, and measure the total-variation
distance between the selected-output distribution and unwatermarked BoN
(Figure~\ref{fig:toy}). At $N{=}1$ the shift is zero for all $\rho$ (the classical
guarantee); for $N>1$ it grows with $N$ (TV $0.16\to0.55$ at $\rho{=}0.9$),
the candidate set's effective diversity collapses ($4.9\to2.5$ distinct answers
of $5$ at $N{=}16$), and the selected reward drops accordingly. Independent
candidates ($\rho{=}0$, our fix) keep the curve at zero.
The mechanism admits a closed form:

\begin{lemma}[Correlation attenuates test-time scaling]\label{lem:attenuation}
Let candidate rewards be exchangeable Gaussian with mean $\mu$, variance
$\sigma^2$ and pairwise correlation $\rho\in[0,1]$, and let
$m_N=\mathbb E[\max_{i\le N}Z_i]$ for i.i.d.\ standard normals
($m_N\sim\sqrt{2\ln N}$). Then the expected reward of hard-BoN is
$\mu+\sigma\sqrt{1-\rho}\,m_N$: the independent system's selection gain is
attenuated by exactly $\sqrt{1-\rho}$, so the correlated pool trails
independent BoN by $(1-\sqrt{1-\rho}\,)\,\sigma\,m_N$ --- increasing in both
$\rho$ and $N$, and equal to the entire gain at $\rho=1$ (the shared-key
collapse). Equivalently, the normalized \emph{scaling efficiency}
$\big(\mathbb E[R_{\mathrm{selected}}]-\mathbb E[R_{\mathrm{single}}]\big)/
\big(\mathbb E[R_{\mathrm{indep.\ BoN}}]-\mathbb E[R_{\mathrm{single}}]\big)$
equals $\sqrt{1-\rho}$ under this model: correlation taxes precisely the
return on additional test-time compute. Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{lemma}

\section{$N$-Selection-Closed Distortion-Freeness}\label{sec:theory}

\begin{definition}[$N$-selection-closed]\label{def:closure}
A watermarked sampler is \emph{exactly $N$-selection-closed} \dftext{} if, for
every prompt $x$, the joint law of its $N$ candidates equals
$Q_x^{\otimes N}$, the law of $N$ i.i.d.\ unwatermarked samples. It is
\emph{computationally} $N$-selection-closed if the two joint laws are
computationally indistinguishable. Statements below are for the exact notion;
each transfers verbatim to the computational one by composing the
distinguisher with the (efficient) selector.
\end{definition}

\textbf{Standing assumptions.} Selection operators observe the candidates and
auxiliary randomness independent of the watermark key; base samplers depend on
their key only through the keyed noise stream; the verifier knows $N$ or an
upper bound.

\begin{theorem}[Characterization of selection-closure]\label{thm:closure}
The following are equivalent:
(i) the joint law of the candidates equals $Q_x^{\otimes N}$ for every prompt;
(ii) for \emph{every} (possibly randomized) selection operator $S$ satisfying
the standing assumptions, the law of the selected output $Y_S$ equals that of
$S$ applied to unwatermarked i.i.d.\ candidates.
Moreover, marginal preservation alone does not imply (ii) for any $N\ge2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(i)$\Rightarrow$(ii): $Y_S$ is a measurable functional of the candidate tuple
and independent auxiliary randomness; equal joint laws have equal pushforwards.
(ii)$\Rightarrow$(i): probe selectors of the form ``pick $i$ if the other
coordinates fall in prescribed sets, else pick $j$'' identify every
finite-dimensional joint by induction on its order (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).
The final claim is witnessed by \S\ref{sec:toy}: exact marginals, correlated
joints, biased selection.
\end{proof}

The universal selector class is what yields an exact characterization; the
positive direction directly covers practical inference-time selectors ---
hard and soft Best-of-$N$, reward reranking, verifier selection, rejection
sampling, filter-and-sample, and randomized voting rules --- provided the
selector does not access the watermark key.

The scope of the standard guarantee is worth fixing precisely, since our
argument turns on it. The definitions in the literature are per-generation
and key-averaged: \citet{kuditipudi2023robust} (Definition~1) require
$\Pr_\xi\big(\Gamma(\xi,\mu)=y\big)=\mu(y)$ --- one decode,
probability over the watermark randomness $\xi$ --- and
\citet{keycollision2024} name exactly this notion \emph{weakly}
distortion-free, reserving \emph{strongly} for the joint law of several
generations. Nothing in the weak form constrains a candidate pool drawn
under one key realization.

Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} states what must hold. The next result explains
why no shared-key design can provide it: distortion-freeness constrains the
key-\emph{averaged} output law --- a first moment --- while detection
requires the key to leave a distributional trace, and the two demands collide
at the second moment.

\begin{proposition}[Detectability excludes closure under a shared key]\label{prop:moments}
Let $K\sim\kappa$ be the key, $P_K$ the key-conditional output law at a
fixed prompt, and let the $N$ candidates be conditionally i.i.d.\ given $K$
(deterministic decoders are the degenerate case $P_K=\delta_{f(K,x)}$;
sharing randomness beyond the key only increases dependence).
\textbf{(i)} Marginal distortion-freeness is the first-moment constraint
$\mathbb E_K[P_K]=Q_x$, while the candidate pool follows
$\mathbb E_K[P_K^{\otimes N}]$ --- the $N$-th moment of the random measure
$P_K$. Test-time scaling therefore evaluates moments that the single-sample
certification never constrained.
\textbf{(ii)} The following are equivalent:
(a) $\mathbb E_K[P_K^{\otimes N}]=Q_x^{\otimes N}$ for some $N\ge2$;
(b) the same for every $N$;
(c) $P_K=Q_x$ for $\kappa$-a.e.\ $K$.
Under (c) the watermark leaves no distributional trace: even a verifier who
knows $K$ has zero advantage from any number of samples.
\textbf{(iii)} Hence every detectable watermark fails joint closure under a
shared key; determinism sets only the \emph{magnitude}, via
\[
\mathrm{Cov}\big(g(Y_i),g(Y_j)\big)
=\mathrm{Var}_K\big(\mathbb E[g(Y)\mid K]\big)\qquad(i\ne j)
\]
for every key-independent statistic $g$: the equicorrelation of
Lemma~\ref{lem:attenuation} is the intraclass coefficient
$\rho_g=\mathrm{Var}_K(\mathbb E[g\mid K])/\mathrm{Var}(g(Y))$, equal to
$1$ for deterministic decoders (complete collapse) and to $0$ only when the
key does not move the conditional mean of $g$. Proof in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proposition}

The dichotomy sharpens \S\ref{sec:collapse}: the collapse of deterministic
decoders is the extreme point $\rho_g=1$ of a constraint that binds every
detectable watermark. A stochastic sampler sits toward the other end --- for
published KGW at $\delta{=}2$ we measure no attenuation within noise
(Appendix~\ref{app:kgw}) --- but reaching $\rho_g=0$ exactly, for all $g$,
means erasing the key's distributional trace altogether. The only exits are
undetectability or ceasing to share the key across candidates; the lift of
\S\ref{sec:method} is the second exit. Part (ii) recovers, at the
candidate-pool level, the impossibility of strong distortion-freeness under
key collision \citep[Theorem~4.10]{keycollision2024}; parts (i) and (iii)
are the selection-facing additions. The dichotomy, however, is only the
endpoint --- the next result replaces it with a quantitative law for the
whole range between ``no watermark'' and ``complete collapse''.

\begin{proposition}[The key-covariance operator and the size of the drift]\label{prop:drift}
Assume the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:moments} with marginal
distortion-freeness, $P_K\ll Q_x$ and
$f_K=\mathrm dP_K/\mathrm dQ_x\in L^2(Q_x)$. Define the
\emph{key-covariance operator} $C=\mathbb E_K[(f_K-1)\otimes(f_K-1)]$ on
$L^2(Q_x)$ and the kernel
$\rho(K,K')=\langle f_K-1,\,f_{K'}-1\rangle_{L^2(Q_x)}$ for independent
copies $K,K'$.
\textbf{(i) One object, three roles.}
$\operatorname{tr}C=\mathbb E_K\big[\chi^2(P_K\Vert Q_x)\big]$ is the
single-sample, key-informed detectability; for any statistic $g$ with
$\mathrm{Var}\,g(Y)=1$ and centering $\bar g=g-\mathbb E_{Q_x}g$, the
intraclass correlation of Proposition~\ref{prop:moments}(iii) is the
quadratic form $\rho_g=\langle\bar g,\,C\bar g\rangle$; and $C=0$ iff
$P_K=Q_x$ a.s.\ (the dichotomy).
\textbf{(ii) Exact drift identity.}
\[
1+\chi^2\big(\mathbb E_K[P_K^{\otimes N}]\,\big\Vert\,Q_x^{\otimes N}\big)
=\mathbb E_{K,K'}\big[(1+\rho(K,K'))^N\big].
\]
Marginal distortion-freeness is equivalent to $\mathbb E_K[f_K]=1$, which
forces $\mathbb E_{K,K'}[\rho]=0$ and cancels the first-order ($O(N)$)
term of the expansion --- that cancellation is precisely, and only, what the
marginal certification buys.
\textbf{(iii) Quadratic growth and a selector-uniform bound.} Let
$\rho_{\max}=\operatorname{ess\,sup}|\rho(K,K')|$. If
$N\rho_{\max}\le\tfrac12$, then
\[
\chi^2=\binom N2\,\Vert C\Vert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\,(1+\theta),
\qquad|\theta|\le N\rho_{\max},
\]
and by data processing every key-independent selector obeys
\[
\mathrm{TV}\big(\text{selected law},\;\text{reference selected law}\big)
\;\le\;\mathrm{TV}\big(\mathbb E_K[P_K^{\otimes N}],\,Q_x^{\otimes N}\big)
\;\le\;\tfrac12\sqrt{\chi^2}\;\le\;\tfrac N2\,\Vert C\Vert_{\mathrm{HS}} .
\]
Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proposition}

In words: under marginal distortion-freeness the joint drift is
second-moment driven; it grows quadratically in $N$ on the $\chi^2$ scale
(linearly on the TV scale); and one operator controls everything ---
its trace is detectability, its quadratic forms are the correlations that
attenuate test-time scaling (Lemma~\ref{lem:attenuation}), its
Hilbert--Schmidt norm sets the drift rate, and it vanishes exactly on the
undetectable schemes. The small-$\rho$ regime covers schemes with fresh
per-step randomness (stochastic KGW, random offsets); deterministic decoders
sit at the opposite extreme ($f_K$ a point-mass density), where the collapse
analysis of \S\ref{sec:collapse} applies instead.

\begin{proposition}[Hierarchy]\label{prop:hierarchy}
As classes of watermarked samplers,
\[
\{\text{undetectable}\}\;\subsetneq\;
\{\text{$N$-selection-closed}\}\;\subsetneq\;
\{\text{marginally \dftext}\}:
\]
(i) closure implies marginal \dftext{}ness (take the constant selector);
(ii) the converse fails --- the shared-key context-hash family is marginally
\dftext{} yet not even $2$-selection-closed;
(iii) undetectability under adaptive queries \citep{christ2023undetectable}
implies $N$-selection-closedness computationally for every polynomial $N$;
(iv) the converse fails --- the lifted family is $N$-selection-closed yet
key-free distinguishable from response-collision statistics. Proofs in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proposition}

\section{Candidate-Separated Watermarking}\label{sec:method}
This section is deliberately scheme-agnostic: we define a transform on
\emph{arbitrary} keyed watermarking schemes and prove its guarantees once, for
all schemes and all selectors. No new watermarking algorithm is introduced;
existing schemes are lifted unmodified.

\subsection{The candidate-separated lift $\mathrm{JC}_N$}
A \emph{keyed sampler} $W$ is any watermarked decoder whose per-step
randomness is a deterministic (pseudorandom) function of a key $k$ and the
generation state, and whose key-dependence is \emph{only} through this noise
stream --- e.g., context-hash Gumbel/Aaronson, exponential/inverse-transform
sampling, tournament sampling, or biased-logit schemes with keyed sampling.
The \emph{jointly-coupled lift} $\mathrm{JC}_N(W)$ generates candidate
$i\in[N]$ by running $W$ \emph{unmodified} under the domain-separated subkey
$\mathrm{SubKey}_i=\mathrm{PRF}(\mathrm{MasterKey},\,i)$ \citep{ggm1986}; in implementations
this is one line --- mix the candidate index into the seed-derivation chain.
One master key is stored; the derivation is free. (``Jointly coupled'' refers
to \emph{controlling} the full joint candidate law, not to increasing
dependence among candidates.)

\begin{theorem}[Exact independent-key lift]\label{thm:exactlift}
Let $W$ be a keyed sampler with key-averaged output law $\mu_W$. Draw
$K_1,\dots,K_N$ i.i.d.\ from the key distribution and generate candidate $i$
by running $W$ unmodified under $K_i$. Then
$(Y_1,\dots,Y_N)\sim\mu_W^{\otimes N}$ \emph{exactly} --- for any base
scheme $W$, distortion-free or not. If $\mu_W=Q_x$ for every prompt, the
construction is exactly $N$-selection-closed, and by
Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} the post-selection output equals unwatermarked
Best-of-$N$ under every key-independent selector.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Independent keys make the candidates independent runs of $W$; each marginal is
the key-averaged law $\mu_W$. Full proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Single-master-key realization]\label{thm:lifting}
Let $K_i=\mathrm{PRF}(K_{\mathrm{master}},\,i)$ as in $\mathrm{JC}_N$. Under
PRF security the candidate tuple is computationally indistinguishable from
$\mu_W^{\otimes N}$; consequently every \emph{efficient} key-independent
selector produces an output computationally indistinguishable from the same
selector applied to independent samples of $\mu_W$, and marginally
distortion-free $W$ becomes \emph{computationally} $N$-selection-closed. Any
detector for $W$ lifts to a detector for $\mathrm{JC}_N(W)$ (the union
wrapper of \S\ref{sec:detection}) with a once-per-sequence multiplicity cost.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
A distinguisher against the candidate tuple composed with the
stream-generation map is a distinguisher against the PRF at $N$ distinct
inputs; conclude with Theorem~\ref{thm:exactlift} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}. Detection is Proposition~\ref{prop:union}. Full
proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Key management, not impossibility]\label{rem:price}
Exact joint closure and detectability are simultaneously achievable: sample
$N$ independent keys, store them all, and run the union detector over the
stored keys. What the PRF buys is \emph{compactness} --- one short master key,
any number of subkeys derivable on demand, no per-candidate key storage and no
pre-commitment to $N$ --- at the price of the exact tier weakening to the
computational one with gap $\varepsilon$. The design axis is therefore
\emph{independent key storage} (exact closure, $O(N)$ key material, $N$ fixed
in advance) versus a \emph{compact master key} (computational closure, $O(1)$
key material, $N$ unbounded); $\mathrm{JC}_N$ is the compact end of this
axis.
\end{remark}

A natural worry about the compact end is information-theoretic: the $N$
subkeys are a deterministic function of one $s$-bit master key, so their
joint entropy is at most $s$, versus $Ns$ for independently stored keys. Is
something lost? The next proposition gives the exact account: the deficit is
real, it is shared by \emph{every} compact-key design, it vanishes in the
idealized model in which the base guarantee itself is stated, and in the
concrete model it costs exactly one PRF-security term.

\begin{proposition}[Information-theoretic account of the master key]\label{prop:itcost}
Let $Q_x$ be the reference candidate law at prompt $x$ and $H_\infty(Q_x)$
its min-entropy over full candidates; consider decoders that are
deterministic given their key material (our scheme family).
\textbf{(i) (Compact keys cannot be statistically closed.)} If the total key
material is $s$ bits, the candidate-pool law $P_x$ of \emph{any} such scheme
satisfies
\[
\mathrm{TV}\big(P_x,\;Q_x^{\otimes N}\big)\;\ge\;1-2^{\,s-N\,H_\infty(Q_x)} .
\]
Hence closure up to total variation $\delta$ forces
$s\ge N\,H_\infty(Q_x)+\log_2(1-\delta)$: the stored randomness must
essentially match the min-entropy of the content it imitates. Taking $N{=}1$,
the same bound shows that the base scheme's \emph{marginal}
distortion-freeness is itself only computational once the $T$-token content
entropy exceeds $s$ --- the lift occupies exactly the tier the base scheme
already occupies.
\textbf{(ii) (In the ideal-noise model the derivation is lossless.)} In the
random-function idealization of the per-step noise --- the model in which the
base scheme's marginal guarantee is exact --- the noise processes read by
different candidates live on disjoint domains $\{i\}\times\mathcal C$ and
are therefore \emph{exactly} mutually independent, so $\mathrm{JC}_N$ is
exactly $N$-selection-closed with no loss whatsoever: the operative resource
is domain disjointness, not key entropy. (The shared-key collapse, by
contrast, is model-independent: identical queries return identical noise in
every model.)
\textbf{(iii) (Concrete security: one term, one hybrid.)} For any adversary
running in time $t$ that adaptively chooses prompts, applies any efficient
key-independent selector, and evaluates any $[0,1]$-bounded functional of the
candidate pool or released output, the difference between $\mathrm{JC}_N$
and the independent-key construction is at most
$\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{prf}}(t+O(q),\,q)$, with $q$ the total number of
noise queries --- a single hybrid, no factor of $N$. Consequently
\[
\mathrm{dist}\big(P_x,\;Q_x^{\otimes N}\big)\;\le\;
\underbrace{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{prf}}}_{\text{coupling (the lift)}}
\;+\;N\cdot\underbrace{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{base}}}_{\text{marginal (inherited)}}
\]
in the computational metric: subkey derivation contributes exactly one term;
every other deviation is inherited from the base scheme, not created by the
lift. Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proposition}

Numerically, part (iii) is vacuous at experimental scale in the best sense:
an AES-based PRF with $s{=}128$, $q\le2^{40}$ queries and $t\le2^{80}$ time
gives $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{prf}}\le2^{-47}\approx10^{-14}$ --- thirteen
orders of magnitude below the resolution of our confidence intervals
($\sim10^{-4}$) --- and the measured oracle-vs-PRF difference of $-0.0003$
(CI $[-0.0015,0.0009]$, Table~\ref{tab:main}) is the matching empirical cap.
Part (i) shows the entropy deficit behind the worry is not a defect of PRF
derivation: any scheme with keys shorter than content entropy has it, and
only the computational tier is available there.

Theorem~\ref{thm:exactlift} is worth emphasizing: it holds for schemes that
are \emph{not} distortion-free (e.g., biased-logit families with keyed
sampling) --- the lift restores candidate i.i.d.-ness over the scheme's own
marginal, removing the selection-induced pathology while leaving any marginal
bias unchanged. For distortion-free $W$ it is the full closure guarantee. As a
byproduct, strict (argmax) decoders can be used again --- no softening --- so
each candidate individually inherits the base scheme's single-sample guarantee
unchanged.

\subsection{Detection: the union wrapper}\label{sec:detection}
The verifier knows the master key and $N$ (an upper bound suffices), not the
selected index. The \emph{union wrapper} is scheme-agnostic --- it runs the base
scheme's own detector under each derived subkey. We analyze it under a
location-shift signal model:
at a watermarked token the correct subkey's score is
$\mathrm{Gumbel}(\Delta,1)$, where $\Delta$ is the per-token signal (for uniform
logits over a nucleus of size $V$, exactly $\Delta=\ln V$; in general $\Delta$
grows with the local entropy), while every other subkey sees a null
$\mathrm{Gumbel}(0,1)$.

\textbf{Union detector.} Run the standard single-key test under each subkey and
report the minimum p-value, Bonferroni-corrected by $N$. The correct subkey
retains the \emph{full} per-token signal $\Delta$; the correction is a
once-per-sequence threshold shift of $\approx\sqrt{2\ln N}$ in z-score, which is
$o(1)$ relative to the $\sqrt{T}\,\Delta$ signal
(Proposition~\ref{prop:union}). As a byproduct, the argmax over subkeys recovers
the selected candidate's index --- an audit log of the selection, at no extra cost
(cf.\ message decoding in multi-bit watermarking \citealp{fernandez2023three}).

The correction is a once-per-sequence quantity while the signal accrues per
token, and this asymmetry can be made precise. On the null side no
approximation is needed at all; on the signal side the cost, measured per
token, vanishes with text length:

\begin{theorem}[Vanishing per-token cost of index-blind detection]\label{thm:vanish}
Fix $N$ and $\alpha\in(0,\tfrac12]$, and let the standing assumptions and
the signal model above hold. Let $z_i=\Phi^{-1}(1-p_i)$ be the reported
single-key z-scores and $z_{\mathrm{union}}=\Phi^{-1}\!\big(1-\min(1,N\min_i p_i)\big)$.
\textbf{(i) (Exact null; extreme-value law.)} On unwatermarked text of
\emph{any} length $T$, the $p_i$ are i.i.d.\ exactly uniform, so the $z_i$
are i.i.d.\ exactly standard normal and $\max_i z_i$ has c.d.f.\
$\Phi(z)^N$ at every finite $T$; as $N\to\infty$,
$\sqrt{2\ln N}\,(\max_i z_i-b_N)$ converges to a standard Gumbel law with
$b_N=\sqrt{2\ln N}-\tfrac{\ln\ln N+\ln4\pi}{2\sqrt{2\ln N}}$. The union
test is exactly level-$\alpha$ at every $T$, with realized family-wise level
$1-(1-\alpha/N)^N\uparrow1-e^{-\alpha}\ge\alpha-\alpha^2/2$: the
Bonferroni correction is tight up to $O(\alpha^2)$, so the $\sqrt{2\ln N}$
threshold is not an artifact of a loose union bound.
\textbf{(ii) (Minimal detectable per-token signal.)} The smallest per-token
score shift detectable at level $\alpha$ with power $1-\beta$ satisfies
\[
c^*_N(T)-c^*_1(T)\;\le\;\sigma_g\big(z_{\alpha/N}-z_\alpha\big)\,T^{-1/2}+o(T^{-1/2}),
\qquad z_{\alpha/N}-z_\alpha\le\sqrt{2\ln(N/\alpha)},
\]
with $z_{\alpha/N}-z_\alpha=\sqrt{2\ln N}\,(1+o(1))$ as $N\to\infty$; by
the needle-in-a-haystack lower bound this order is not improvable. Hence the
per-token price of not knowing the index is $O\big(\sqrt{\ln N}\,T^{-1/2}\big)\to0$.
\textbf{(iii) (Realized penalty on strong signals.)} If the correct-subkey
z-score satisfies $z_T=\Theta_P(\sqrt T)\to\infty$, then
\[
z_T-z_{\mathrm{union}}\;=\;\frac{\ln N}{z_T}\,\big(1+o_P(1)\big)\;=\;\Theta_P\big(T^{-1/2}\big).
\]
Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:detection}.
\end{theorem}

At our operating point ($T\approx195$ scored tokens, $N{=}16$, signal
$z\approx8$) part~(iii) predicts a penalty of $\ln16/8\approx0.35$
z-scores; the observed gap between the shared-key arm's base-key z-score and
the candidate-separated arm's union z-score is $\approx0.3$
(Table~\ref{tab:main}), and the simulation of Figure~\ref{fig:detectors}
confirms that the union detector tracks the correct-subkey oracle up to
exactly this threshold shift.

The union wrapper introduces two distinct costs, which must not be conflated
with each other or with selection effects. \emph{Statistically}, testing $N$
subkeys raises the rejection threshold by
$\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/N)-\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)=\Theta(\sqrt{\log N})$ under a
normal approximation, with $\sqrt{2\ln N}$ the asymptotic leading order; this
penalty is order-optimal --- detecting one elevated stream among $N$
independent nulls is the classical needle-in-a-haystack problem, for which no
index-blind level-$\alpha$ test can improve on the max test's threshold order
\citep{ingster1997,donoho2004higher}. \emph{Computationally}, detection
requires $N$ evaluations of the base detector, which are independent and
parallelizable. Separately from both, \emph{reward selection itself} tilts
the released text toward weakly watermarked candidates --- a loss that exists
for any token-level watermark under any key design and is orthogonal to the
multiplicity correction:

\begin{proposition}[Selection tilt]\label{prop:tilt}
If each candidate's (reward, per-text signal) pair is bivariate Gaussian with
correlation $\rho_{rs}$, i.i.d.\ across candidates, then hard-BoN selection
shifts the released signal by
$\mathbb E[z_{i^\ast}]-\mu_z=\rho_{rs}\,\sigma_z\,m_N$. With $\rho_{rs}<0$
(measured $\approx-0.5$) the post-selection signal declines at the
$\sqrt{2\ln N}$ rate --- the shape of Figure~\ref{fig:realshift} (right).
Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proposition}

Like the multiplicity correction, the tilt is a once-per-sequence, $O(1)$
quantity on the z-scale ($\sigma_z$ does not grow with text length, and
$m_N$ grows only as $\sqrt{2\ln N}$), while the signal grows as
$\Theta(\sqrt T)$: measured per token, \emph{every} selection-era
detection cost --- threshold inflation and tilt alike --- vanishes as
$O(T^{-1/2})$.

\subsection{Instantiations}\label{sec:instantiations}
The framework specializes with no per-scheme work.
\emph{Gumbel/Aaronson} (our flagship, \S\ref{sec:experiments}): candidate $i$
runs strict argmax decoding with noise seeded by
$(\mathrm{SubKey}_i,\mathrm{ctx})$; the union wrapper reuses the standard
$\Gamma$-null detector.
\emph{Exponential / permute-and-flip samplers} \citep{zhao2024permute}: identical
treatment --- the noise is the same exponential family, so the detector carries
over verbatim.
\emph{Inverse-transform / random-offset schemes} \citep{kuditipudi2023robust}:
the lift replaces the random offset's approximate decorrelation with exact
(computational) independence while keeping their alignment detector per subkey.
\emph{Biased-logit schemes} (KGW): Theorem~\ref{thm:exactlift} applies ---
candidates become i.i.d.\ over the biased marginal, restoring ordinary BoN
behavior; full closure is vacuous since the base scheme is not
distortion-free.

calibrates exactly as in the Gumbel case (FPR $0.055/0.011$ at nominal
$0.05/0.01$; lifted text is null under the base key, $0.054$). One honest
difference: after reward selection the retained signal is weaker than for
Gumbel (median $z$ $4.27$, TPR $0.830$ at FPR $0.01$) --- the
reward--watermark anticorrelation is more pronounced for this scheme.

\textbf{Third scheme family (biased-logit KGW).}
Published KGW samples with fresh randomness, so its pool does not collapse,
and at $\delta{=}2$ we measure no scaling attenuation --- the negative
control predicted by Proposition~\ref{prop:moments}(iii); its costs are
marginal (it is not distortion-free). Derandomizing the sampler --- the
design point of deterministic deployments --- produces the collapse, and
separating the sampling stream per candidate repairs it \emph{with the
detector unchanged}: KGW's detector reads only the greenlist, which stays on
the base key, so this family pays no union cost at all. Numbers in
Appendix~\ref{app:kgw}.

\textbf{Reasoning-side evaluation (GSM8K).}
The same failure and repair appear on the \emph{accuracy} axis of test-time
scaling: under the shared key, pass@16 and self-consistency collapse to the
single-sample level (a loss of $39$ and $27$ accuracy points respectively),
candidate separation restores both to the unwatermarked level, and the
softened workaround shows a statistically significant single-sample accuracy
tax that the reward axis does not expose. Detection on this short,
low-entropy domain is weaker for \emph{every} scheme, and the verification
tier matters: a provider who retains the selected index pays no multiplicity
cost, while index-blind verification pays the needle-in-a-haystack fee of
\S\ref{sec:detection}. Full table and discussion in
Appendix~\ref{app:gsm}.

\textbf{Candidate diversity.}
Within-prompt statistics confirm closure at the sample level: the subkey arm is
statistically indistinguishable from unwatermarked sampling
(unique fraction $1.00$ vs.\ $1.00$, $3$-gram Jaccard $0.038$ vs.\ $0.039$,
shared word-prefix $1.10$ vs.\ $1.09$), the strict shared key is degenerate
(unique $0.06$, Jaccard $1.00$, shared prefix $154$ words --- the entire
generation), and the softened workaround retains a residual $2.2\times$ prefix
correlation ($2.45$ vs.\ $1.09$ words): softening decorrelates incompletely
\emph{and} sacrifices the exact marginal certification.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{Security.} Multiple keys trade off against stealing/removal attacks
\citep{jovanovic2024stealing, nofreelunch2024}: more keys hide patterns better but
ease distribution estimation. Our setting is mild --- $N$ is small (2--16), the
subkeys are per-\emph{candidate} while only one candidate is released per query,
diluting an attacker's per-subkey sample budget by $N$ --- but the standard
trade-offs apply and deserve study.
\textbf{Scope.} Subkeys restore \emph{distributional} correctness; they do not
change the reward--watermark anticorrelation of the text itself (selection still
prefers weakly watermarked candidates; $z$ retained $79$--$95\%$ across heads),
which is an orthogonal, entropy-driven phenomenon. Intra-sequence key reuse
\citep{keycollision2024} is likewise orthogonal and composable with our fix.

The tilt, finally, is removable in principle: the exponential race that
drives keyed samplers has a time coordinate (the winning arrival time) that
is exactly independent of the emitted token, so a verifier with model access
can read the watermark from a statistic whose law reward selection provably
cannot shift. We do not pursue this here: it requires the generating model,
the prompt, and repeated-context masking at verification, and the cost it
recovers is already $O(1)$ per sequence and vanishing per token.

\section{Limitations}
Key-dependent selectors are outside the guarantee (and demonstrably break
closure, Appendix~\ref{app:exp}). Candidate separation restores the intended
candidate law but does not remove the reward--watermark anticorrelation:
reward selection may still prefer candidates with weaker watermark evidence.
Computational closure is weaker than adaptive multi-query undetectability
(Proposition~\ref{prop:hierarchy}); by
Proposition~\ref{prop:itcost}(i) the computational tier is forced for any
compact-key design --- statistical closure would require stored key material
comparable to the content entropy. Union detection scales linearly in the
number of candidate subkeys, and the verifier must know $N$ or an upper bound.
The study focuses on deterministic keyed decoder families; the main
evaluation uses one generator family (instruction-tuned and base variants),
one reward model, and one prompt distribution, with a second decoder family,
a biased-logit family, and a mathematical-reasoning benchmark
(Appendix~\ref{app:gsm}) as replications. Detection on short, low-entropy
domains is weak for every scheme we test, and index-blind verification
compounds this with the multiplicity fee.

\section{Conclusion}
Distortion-free watermarking is usually defined for one generation, while
inference-time alignment and test-time scaling act on a set of generations. We
showed that this distinction is consequential: exact candidate marginals can
coexist with a correlated joint law that changes the post-selection
distribution, and shared deterministic randomness can eliminate the entire
Best-of-$N$ gain. The appropriate guarantee is therefore selection-level and
joint: the candidate pool should match independent samples from the reference
model. Candidate-specific keys provide a minimal correction --- independent
keys give exact selection closure; PRF-derived subkeys give a compact
computational realization under one master key --- and, with a union detector,
restore the reward and diversity benefits of selection-based inference while
retaining the base watermark's marginal guarantee. The broader lesson is that
watermarking should be evaluated not only at $N{=}1$, but along the same
test-time scaling curves used for alignment: a watermark is compatible with
Best-of-$N$ only when its guarantee survives selection.

\subsection{Notation and setting}
Fix a prompt $x$. Let $Q_x$ denote the unwatermarked single-sample law over
completions and $Q_x^{\otimes N}$ the law of $N$ i.i.d.\ samples. A watermarked
sampler with key $k\sim\mathcal K$ produces a candidate tuple
$\mathbf Y=(Y_1,\dots,Y_N)$ with joint law $P_x^{N}$ (all statements about $P$
are averaged over the key unless stated otherwise). A \emph{selection operator}
is a measurable map $S:\mathcal Y^{N}\times\mathcal U\to[N]$, where $U\sim\mu$ is
auxiliary randomness (e.g., a reward model's scores and tie-breaking)
independent of the watermark key; the released output is $Y_S$.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}}
\textbf{Sufficiency (exact form).}
The map $\Phi:(\mathbf y,u)\mapsto y_{S(\mathbf y,u)}$ is measurable, and the law
of the released output is the pushforward $\Phi_\#(P_x^{N}\otimes\mu)$. If
$P_x^{N}=Q_x^{\otimes N}$ then
$\Phi_\#(P_x^{N}\otimes\mu)=\Phi_\#(Q_x^{\otimes N}\otimes\mu)$, which is exactly
the law of unwatermarked Best-of-$N$ under the same selector. Since $\Phi$ was
arbitrary, closure holds for \emph{every} selector simultaneously.

\textbf{Sufficiency (computational form).}
Suppose $P_x^{N}$ and $Q_x^{\otimes N}$ are computationally indistinguishable and
$S$ runs in polynomial time. Any polynomial-time distinguisher $D$ acting on the
released output induces the distinguisher $D\circ\Phi$ acting on the candidate
tuple (sampling $U$ internally), with identical advantage. Hence the released
outputs are indistinguishable.

\textbf{Necessity (probe selectors).}
Assume (ii). Constant selectors $S\equiv i$ force every marginal to equal
$Q_x$. We show by induction on $k$ that every $k$-dimensional joint
factorizes. For $k=2$ and $i\ne j$, use the probe ``$S=i$ if $Y_j\in B$, else
$j$'': its output law on $A$ is
$P(Y_i\in A,Y_j\in B)+P(Y_j\in A,Y_j\notin B)$; the second term equals
$Q_x(A\cap B^c)$ by the marginal step, and the i.i.d.\ reference value is
$Q_x(A)Q_x(B)+Q_x(A\cap B^c)$, so $P(Y_i\in A,Y_j\in B)=Q_x(A)Q_x(B)$.
For the step $k{-}1\to k$, fix distinct coordinates $i,t_1,\dots,t_{k-1}$ and
sets $A,B_1,\dots,B_{k-1}$; write $E=\bigcap_m\{Y_{t_m}\in B_m\}$, pick
$j=t_1$, and use the probe ``$S=i$ if $E$, else $j$''. Its output law on $A$
is $P(Y_i\in A,E)+P(Y_j\in A,E^c)$; the second term is
$P(Y_j\in A)-P(Y_j\in A,E)$, and $P(Y_j\in A,E)$ merges the two occurrences
of $Y_j$ into $\{Y_j\in A\cap B_1\}$, hence is a $(k{-}1)$-dimensional joint,
which factorizes by induction and matches the i.i.d.\ reference. Subtracting
yields $P(Y_i\in A,E)=Q_x(A)\prod_m Q_x(B_m)$. Cylinder sets generate the
product $\sigma$-algebra, so the joint equals $Q_x^{\otimes N}$. The
computational version follows by quantitative bookkeeping of the same probes.
\qed

\textbf{Insufficiency of marginal preservation.}
The following minimal example shows that exact marginals give no control
whatsoever over the selected output; it is the formal core of
Figure~\ref{fig:toy} and of the real-model collapse in
Table~\ref{tab:main}.

\begin{example}[Comonotone candidates]\label{ex:comonotone}
Let the outcome space be $\{0,1\}$ with $Q_x(\{1\})=\tfrac12$, and let the reward
satisfy $r(1)>r(0)$ so that the selector returns $1$ whenever some candidate
equals $1$. Consider the comonotone coupling $Y_1=\dots=Y_N$ with
$Y_1\sim Q_x$ --- every marginal is exact. Unwatermarked Best-of-$N$ releases $1$
with probability $1-2^{-N}$; the comonotone sampler releases $1$ with probability
$\tfrac12$. The total-variation shift of the released output is therefore
\[
\mathrm{TV}=\tfrac12-2^{-N}\;\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}\;\tfrac12 ,
\]
monotonically increasing in $N$. The shared-key deterministic scheme of
\S\ref{sec:collapse} realizes exactly this coupling (all candidates identical),
so the bound is attained by the shared-key instantiation, not only in the worst case. \qed
\end{example}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:moments}}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{(i)} Conditional independence gives, for measurable
$A_1,\dots,A_N$,
$\Pr[Y_1\in A_1,\dots,Y_N\in A_N]=\mathbb E_K\prod_{i\le N}P_K(A_i)$;
the marginal constraint is the case $N{=}1$.
\textbf{(ii)} (a)$\Rightarrow$(c): projecting onto two coordinates reduces
any $N\ge2$ to $N{=}2$. The outcome space (finite token sequences) is
countable; fix a sequence $y$ and take $A_1=A_2=\{y\}$:
$\mathbb E[P_K(y)^2]=Q_x(y)^2$, while the one-coordinate projection gives
$\mathbb E[P_K(y)]=Q_x(y)$; hence $\mathrm{Var}_K(P_K(y))=0$ and
$P_K(y)=Q_x(y)$ almost surely. A countable union of null sets yields
$P_K=Q_x$ a.s. (c)$\Rightarrow$(b)$\Rightarrow$(a) are immediate. For the
detection claim: on the event $P_K=Q_x$, the conditional law of any number
of samples equals the null law, so every test statistic has the same
distribution under both hypotheses.
\textbf{(iii)} If the scheme is not marginally distortion-free, closure
fails already at one coordinate. If it is, and it is detectable, then
$P_K\ne Q_x$ on a key set of positive measure, so for some sequence $y$ the
variable $P_K(y)$ is nonconstant with mean $Q_x(y)$; taking
$g=\mathbf 1\{Y=y\}$ gives $\mathrm{Var}_K(\mathbb E[g\mid K])>0$, and
the joint law differs from the product. The covariance identity is
conditional independence plus the tower rule:
$\mathbb E[g(Y_i)g(Y_j)]=\mathbb E_K[\mathbb E[g\mid K]^2]$ for
$i\ne j$. For a deterministic decoder, $\mathbb E[g\mid K]=g(f(K,x))$, so
$\mathrm{Var}_K(\mathbb E[g\mid K])=\mathrm{Var}(g(Y))$ and $\rho_g=1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:drift}}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{(i)} $\operatorname{tr}C=\mathbb E_K\Vert f_K-1\Vert^2
=\mathbb E_K\int(f_K-1)^2\,\mathrm dQ_x=\mathbb E_K[\chi^2(P_K\Vert Q_x)]$.
For centered $\bar g$, $\mathbb E[g(Y)\mid K]-\mathbb E_{Q_x}g
=\langle\bar g,f_K-1\rangle$ (using $\mathbb E_K[f_K]=1$), so
$\mathrm{Var}_K(\mathbb E[g\mid K])
=\mathbb E_K\langle\bar g,f_K-1\rangle^2=\langle\bar g,C\bar g\rangle$.
$C=0$ iff $\operatorname{tr}C=0$ iff $f_K=1$ a.s.
\textbf{(ii)} The mixture's density w.r.t.\ $Q_x^{\otimes N}$ is
$h(y_{1:N})=\mathbb E_K\prod_{i\le N}f_K(y_i)$, so
$1+\chi^2=\int h^2\,\mathrm dQ_x^{\otimes N}
=\mathbb E_{K,K'}\prod_{i\le N}\int f_Kf_{K'}\,\mathrm dQ_x
=\mathbb E_{K,K'}\big[(1+\rho(K,K'))^N\big]$ by Fubini
($f_K\in L^2$). Since $K\perp K'$,
$\mathbb E_{K,K'}[\rho]=\Vert\mathbb E_K[f_K]-1\Vert^2$, which vanishes
iff $\mathbb E_K[f_K]=1$, i.e., iff the scheme is marginally
distortion-free; the binomial term $N\,\mathbb E[\rho]$ then drops.
\textbf{(iii)} Expanding and using $\mathbb E[\rho]=0$,
$\chi^2=\binom N2\mathbb E[\rho^2]+\sum_{k\ge3}\binom Nk\mathbb E[\rho^k]$
with $\mathbb E[\rho^2]=\Vert C\Vert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$. Bounding
$|\mathbb E[\rho^k]|\le\mathbb E[\rho^2]\rho_{\max}^{k-2}$ and
$\binom Nk\le\binom N2\,\tfrac{2}{k!}N^{k-2}$ gives
$|\theta|\le2\sum_{k\ge3}(N\rho_{\max})^{k-2}/k!
\le\tfrac13\,\tfrac{N\rho_{\max}}{1-N\rho_{\max}}\le N\rho_{\max}$
for $N\rho_{\max}\le\tfrac12$. The TV chain is data processing (a
key-independent selector is a Markov kernel on the candidate tuple),
Cauchy--Schwarz ($2\,\mathrm{TV}=\int|h-1|\le\sqrt{\chi^2}$), and
$\tfrac12\sqrt{\binom N2(1+\theta)}\le\tfrac N2$ for
$|\theta|\le\tfrac12$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:hierarchy}}
\textbf{(i) $N$-selection-closedness $\Rightarrow$ marginal
distortion-freeness.} Take the selector $S\equiv 1$ (release the first
candidate); closure for this selector is precisely marginal preservation.

\textbf{(ii) Marginal $\not\Rightarrow$ closed.} Example~\ref{ex:comonotone}: the
comonotone sampler is marginally exact and fails $2$-selection-closedness with
$\mathrm{TV}=\tfrac14$.

\textbf{(iii) Undetectability $\Rightarrow$ closure (computational).} An
adversary in the undetectability game of \citet{christ2023undetectable} may issue
the same prompt $N$ times, obtaining (by the multi-query guarantee) a tuple
computationally indistinguishable from $N$ unwatermarked calls. A
polynomial-time distinguisher between $P_x^{N}$ and $Q_x^{\otimes N}$ is thus
literally a distinguisher in the undetectability game, so undetectability
implies indistinguishability of the joints; conclude with
Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}.

\textbf{(iv) Closed $\not\Rightarrow$ undetectable.} The subkey construction with
strict (argmax) decoding is $N$-selection-closed by construction, yet it is not
undetectable: for a fixed candidate index the map from context to token is
deterministic, so an observer issuing many queries with a shared prefix sees
response collisions at a rate far exceeding that of genuine sampling --- a
polynomial-time, key-free distinguisher. (This is a property of the entire
context-hash family, orthogonal to our fix.) Hence the inclusion is strict.
\qed

\subsection{Proofs of Theorems~\ref{thm:exactlift} and~\ref{thm:lifting} and Proposition~\ref{prop:itcost}}
\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:exactlift}.} With $N$ independently sampled keys the
candidates are independent runs of the unmodified base sampler by
construction, hence i.i.d.\ with law $\mu_W$; the joint \emph{equals}
$\mu_W^{\otimes N}$ and every pushforward is preserved exactly. If
$\mu_W=Q_x$, this is Definition~\ref{def:closure} (exact), and
Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} gives closure under every key-independent selector.
\qed

\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:lifting}.} All subkeys are evaluations of one PRF at
distinct points
$(\mathrm{MasterKey},i)$, $i\in[N]$; by PRF security the tuple of derived noise
streams is computationally indistinguishable from $N$ independent truly-random
streams (a single invocation of the PRF game over the $N$ query points; no
hybrid over candidates is needed). Conditioned on independent streams, the
candidates are independent runs of the unmodified base sampler $W$, hence
i.i.d.\ with law $\mu_W$; a distinguisher against the candidate tuple would
therefore distinguish the streams, contradicting PRF security. Neither
theorem assumes anything of $W$ beyond keyed-ness --- in particular no
distortion-freeness. If $\mu_W=Q_x$, indistinguishability from
$Q_x^{\otimes N}$ is Definition~\ref{def:closure} (computational), and the
computational form of Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} yields closure for every
efficient key-independent selector. For detection, the base detector's null is
exact under each wrong subkey (the text is independent of that subkey's
stream), so the union wrapper of
Proposition~\ref{prop:union} applies verbatim, with its once-per-sequence
multiplicity cost and index-recovery byproduct. \qed

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:itcost}]
\textbf{(i)} Fix the prompt $x$. Because the decoder is deterministic given
its key material, the candidate pool is a function $Y=f(K,x)$ of the key
$K$, which takes at most $2^s$ values; hence the support $A$ of $P_x$ has
$|A|\le2^s$. Every atom of $Q_x^{\otimes N}$ has mass at most
$2^{-H_\infty(Q_x^{\otimes N})}=2^{-N H_\infty(Q_x)}$, so
$Q_x^{\otimes N}(A)\le2^{\,s-NH_\infty(Q_x)}$ and
\[
\mathrm{TV}\big(P_x,Q_x^{\otimes N}\big)\ \ge\ P_x(A)-Q_x^{\otimes N}(A)
\ \ge\ 1-2^{\,s-NH_\infty(Q_x)} .
\]
Rearranging gives the storage bound; the $N{=}1$ case is the same bound
applied to a single $T$-token candidate.
\textbf{(ii)} Let $F$ be a uniformly random function on
$[N]\times\mathcal C$. Values of a uniformly random function at distinct
arguments are i.i.d., so the families $\{F(i,c):c\in\mathcal C\}$,
$i\in[N]$, are mutually independent. Candidate $i$'s run of $W$ reads only
$F(i,\cdot)$ (a repeated context \emph{within} candidate $i$ rereads the
same value, exactly as in the base scheme), so the $N$ runs are independent
and each is a run of $W$ with an ideal noise source --- precisely the
independent-key construction of Theorem~\ref{thm:exactlift} in the same
model. Exact closure follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}. Under a shared
key all candidates query the \emph{same} points and receive the same
values, in every model.
\textbf{(iii)} Consider the hybrid in which
$\mathrm{PRF}(K_{\mathrm{master}},\cdot)$ is replaced by a uniformly
random function. Under the hybrid, the $N$ subkeys are values of a random
function at $N$ distinct points --- i.i.d.\ uniform keys --- so the
construction \emph{is} the independent-key lift. Any procedure (adaptive
prompt choice, generation, efficient key-independent selection, bounded
functional evaluation) whose output distribution differs between the two
constructions by $\varepsilon$ is therefore a PRF distinguisher with
advantage $\varepsilon$, $q$ oracle queries, and running time $t+O(q)$;
there is one hybrid step, hence no factor of $N$. For the displayed
decomposition, first replace the coupling (one PRF term), then compare
$\mu_W^{\otimes N}$ with $Q_x^{\otimes N}$ by a candidate-by-candidate
hybrid, which costs $N$ times the base marginal gap; boundedness of the
functional converts indistinguishability into the expectation bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proofs of Lemma~\ref{lem:attenuation} and Proposition~\ref{prop:tilt}}
\emph{Lemma~\ref{lem:attenuation}.} Write the equicorrelated vector as
$X_i=\mu+\sigma(\sqrt{\rho}\,Z_0+\sqrt{1-\rho}\,Z_i)$ with independent
standard normals $Z_0,Z_1,\dots,Z_N$. Then
$\max_i X_i=\mu+\sigma\sqrt{\rho}\,Z_0+\sigma\sqrt{1-\rho}\,\max_i Z_i$, and
taking expectations kills the shared factor:
$\mathbb E[\max_i X_i]=\mu+\sigma\sqrt{1-\rho}\,m_N$. \qed

\emph{Proposition~\ref{prop:tilt}.} Regress the signal on the reward:
$z_i=\mu_z+\rho_{rs}\sigma_z(R_i-\mu_R)/\sigma_R+\eta_i$ with $\eta_i$
independent of the rewards. Selecting $i^\ast=\arg\max_i R_i$ and taking
expectations, $\mathbb E[\eta_{i^\ast}]=0$ (the selection is measurable in the
rewards alone), so
$\mathbb E[z_{i^\ast}]=\mu_z+\rho_{rs}\sigma_z\,
\mathbb E[R_{i^\ast}-\mu_R]/\sigma_R=\mu_z+\rho_{rs}\sigma_z m_N$ for
i.i.d.\ Gaussian rewards. \qed

\subsection{Detection under $N$ subkeys}\label{app:detection}
We work in the location-shift signal model of \S\ref{sec:detection}: token
scores under the correct subkey at watermarked positions are i.i.d.\
$\mathrm{Gumbel}(\Delta,1)$; under any incorrect subkey, and at any position of
unwatermarked text, scores are i.i.d.\ $\mathrm{Gumbel}(0,1)$, independent
across subkeys --- independence holds because distinct subkeys read disjoint
PRF domains (exactly with independently stored keys, computationally with
PRF-derived subkeys);
this is what licenses \emph{exact} null calibration (\v{S}id\'ak or
empirical quantiles) in place of the conservative Bonferroni bound, cf.\ the
matched-FPR evaluation of Appendix~\ref{app:exp}. For uniform logits over a nucleus of size $V$ the model is exact
with $\Delta=\ln V$ (the winning token's noise is the maximum of $V$ i.i.d.\
Gumbels, i.e., $\mathrm{Gumbel}(\ln V,1)$); for peaked distributions it is an approximation.
Throughout, $\gamma$ is Euler's constant,
$\sigma_g^2=\pi^2/6$, and $T$ is the number of scored tokens.

\begin{proposition}[Union detector]\label{prop:union}
For each subkey $i$ let
$z_i=\big(\sum_{t\le T} g_i(x_t)-T\gamma\big)/(\sigma_g\sqrt T)$ and let the
union detector reject when $\max_i z_i$ exceeds the $(1-\alpha/N)$-quantile of
the single-key null. Then:
(a) the false-positive rate is at most $\alpha$ for \emph{any} dependence among
the $z_i$ (Bonferroni), and the null quantile inflation is
$\sqrt{2\ln N}\,(1+o(1))$ in z-units;
(b) under the signal model, the correct subkey satisfies
$\mathbb E[z_{i^*}]=\sqrt T\,\Delta/\sigma_g$, so the power of the union test
equals that of the oracle test up to the additive threshold term:
the z-margin is $\sqrt T\,\Delta/\sigma_g-\sqrt{2\ln N}-z_\alpha$, and the
relative loss vanishes as $T\to\infty$;
(c) $\arg\max_i z_i$ recovers the selected candidate's index with probability
$\to1$ in the same limit.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) is the union bound; the quantile of the maximum of $N$ standard (Gumbel-sum,
asymptotically normal) nulls grows as $\sqrt{2\ln N}$.
(b) Linearity of expectation for the correct subkey; the incorrect subkeys'
maximum is $O_P(\sqrt{2\ln N})$, so for
$\sqrt T\,\Delta/\sigma_g>\sqrt{2\ln N}$ the maximum is attained by the correct
subkey with probability $\to1$, which also gives (c).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:vanish}]
\textbf{(i)} Under the null, every subkey's score sum is a continuous
statistic with a known null law (independent of the key), so its exact
upper-tail p-value $p_i$ is exactly $\mathrm{U}[0,1]$; across subkeys the
$p_i$ are independent because distinct subkeys read disjoint PRF domains.
Hence $z_i=\Phi^{-1}(1-p_i)$ are i.i.d.\ standard normal \emph{exactly},
for every finite $T$, and $\max_i z_i$ has c.d.f.\ $\Phi^N$. The stated
Gumbel limit with norming constants
$a_N=(2\ln N)^{-1/2}$, $b_N=\sqrt{2\ln N}-\frac{\ln\ln N+\ln4\pi}{2\sqrt{2\ln N}}$
is the Fisher--Tippett law for Gaussian maxima. The union test rejects iff
$N\min_i p_i\le\alpha$, an event of exact probability
$1-(1-\alpha/N)^N$, which is $\le\alpha$ (Bonferroni is exactly valid) and
increases to $1-e^{-\alpha}\ge\alpha-\alpha^2/2$ as $N\to\infty$, so the
union bound's slack is $O(\alpha^2)$ uniformly in $N$.
\textbf{(ii)} Under a per-token location shift $c$, the correct-key
z-statistic is asymptotically normal with mean $c\sqrt T/\sigma_g$ and unit
variance. The union test's power is sandwiched between that of the
correct-key test at level $\alpha/N$ (reject only when the correct subkey
clears the corrected threshold) and the same plus the wrong-key
contribution, which is at most $1-(1-\alpha/N)^{N-1}\le\alpha$; both
bounds give a required mean of $z_{\alpha/N}+z_{\beta}+O(\alpha)+o(1)$,
whereas the oracle ($N{=}1$) requires $z_\alpha+z_\beta+o(1)$. Solving
$c\sqrt T/\sigma_g=\text{(required mean)}$ for $c$ gives the display. The
Chernoff quantile bound $z_u\le\sqrt{2\ln(1/u)}$ for $u\le\tfrac12$
(from $Q(z)\le e^{-z^2/2}$, $z\ge0$) with $u=\alpha/N$, together with
$z_\alpha\ge0$ for $\alpha\le\tfrac12$, gives
$z_{\alpha/N}-z_\alpha\le\sqrt{2\ln(N/\alpha)}$; the asymptotics follow
from $z_{\alpha/N}=\sqrt{2\ln(N/\alpha)}(1+o(1))$. Order-optimality in $N$
is the needle-in-a-haystack lower bound \citep{ingster1997,donoho2004higher}.
\textbf{(iii)} The minimum over the $N-1$ wrong keys of i.i.d.\ uniform
p-values is $\Theta_P(1/N)$, bounded away from zero for fixed $N$, while
$p_{i^*}\to_P0$; so with probability $\to1$ the union statistic is
$z_{\mathrm{union}}=\Phi^{-1}(1-Np_{i^*})$. Write $z=z_T$,
$z'=z_{\mathrm{union}}$, $\delta=z-z'\ge0$. Mills-ratio bounds
$\phi(z)(z^{-1}-z^{-3})\le Q(z)\le\phi(z)z^{-1}$ give
$\ln Q(z)=-z^2/2-\ln z-\tfrac12\ln2\pi+O(z^{-2})$; substituting into
$\ln Q(z')=\ln Q(z)+\ln N$ and expanding,
\[
z\delta-\tfrac{\delta^2}{2}+\ln\tfrac{z}{z'}=\ln N+O(z^{-2})
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\delta=\frac{\ln N}{z}\Big(1+O\big((1{+}\ln N)z^{-2}\big)\Big).
\]
With $z_T=\Theta_P(\sqrt T)$ this is $\Theta_P(T^{-1/2})$.
\end{proof}

\section{Experimental Details}\label{app:exp}

\subsection{Configuration}
Generator: LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct (fp16, single A100-40GB per arm); prompts: $128$
from Anthropic HH-RLHF (test split), raw format; $N{=}16$ candidates per prompt,
nucleus sampling $p{=}0.95$, temperature $1.0$, generation length $200$ tokens.
Watermark: Gumbel/Aaronson with context hash over the preceding $h{=}4$ tokens
(hash chain $s\!\leftarrow\!(s\cdot\texttt{salt}+\mathrm{token})\bmod(2^{64}{-}1)$,
master seed $42$); the subkey arm appends a domain-separation step
$s\!\leftarrow\!(s\cdot\texttt{salt}+c_0+i)\bmod(2^{64}{-}1)$ with candidate index
$i$. Reward: ArmoRM-Llama3-8B-v0.1. Detection: per-key score
$\sum_t\log\frac{1}{1-r_{x_t}}$ with exact $\Gamma(n,1)$ null; union arm reports
$\min(1,N\cdot\min_i p_i)$. Selection heads: hard-BoN ($\arg\max$ reward),
soft-/Exp-BoN ($\arg\max$ of $r/\lambda$ plus Gumbel/exponential noise,
$\lambda{=}0.02$). BoN at $N<16$ uses repeated subsampling of the $16$ generated
candidates ($60$--$80$ resamples per prompt). The independent-key oracle arm
draws $16$ salt keys once from OS randomness, stores them, and runs the
\emph{unmodified} sampler with candidate $i$'s salt in the whole hash chain
(no domain-separation step); its detector runs the base test under each
stored key and applies the same Bonferroni union. Its null behavior on
unwatermarked text matches the PRF union exactly (empirical FPR $0.062$ /
$0.013$ at nominal $0.05$/$0.01$, $2048$ texts, vs.\ $0.062$/$0.013$ for the
PRF union). Wall-clock: generation
$\approx13.5$\,min per arm ($128\times16$ candidates, one GPU); union detection is
$N$ single-key passes (embarrassingly parallel), $\approx4.5$\,min for
$2048$ texts at $N{=}16$ on one GPU.

\subsection{Selector family}\label{app:selectors}

that is independent of the watermark key. We test seven key-free selectors
drawn from alignment practice --- single, uniform-random (control),
hard-/soft-/Exp-BoN, rejection sampling (accept the first candidate with reward
above the unwatermarked median, random scan order), and filter+sample (discard
the bottom half by reward, sample uniformly among survivors --- the standard
diversity-preserving softening) --- and two \emph{key-dependent} stress selectors
that deliberately violate the theorem's side condition: adversarial min-$z$
(an evader with a key oracle picks the weakest-watermarked candidate) and
max-$z$ (WaterMax-style watermark boosting). All at $N{=}16$.

\subsection{Selection-head matrix on base and instruct models}\label{app:matrix}
We extend the evaluation to a family of reward-selection heads on two
generators --- the instruction-tuned model used in the main text and its
\emph{base} (pre-alignment) counterpart, LLaMA-3.1-8B --- across seven
watermark arms. Heads: \textbf{single} (one draw); hard \textbf{BoN};
\textbf{SBoN}$(\lambda)$, softmax selection with weights
$\propto e^{r_i/\lambda}$ \citep{verdun2025soft} ($\lambda$ is a
temperature: $\lambda\to0$ recovers BoN); \textbf{BoP}$(\mu)$,
Best-of-Poisson \citep{khalaf2025hedging}: the sample count is
$1+\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu)$, then hard max (computed exactly by order
statistics over the $16$-candidate pool, capped at the pool);
\textbf{BoL}$(b)$, a control head of our own --- $\arg\max$ of rewards
perturbed by $\mathrm{Laplace}(0,b)$ noise --- included to test whether the
perturbation family matters. Reward is the mean (over prompts) of the head's
expected selected reward; median $z$ is the median over prompts of the
expected selected z-score under the arm's own detector; $p{<}.01$ is the
expected detection rate at the nominal $0.01$ level; TPR$^{\mathrm{cal}}$ is
the same rate after threshold calibration --- the threshold is the $0.01$
quantile of the arm's own detector on $2048$ unwatermarked texts from the
same model, so the empirical false-positive rate is $0.01$ by construction.

Three regularities hold in every arm and on both models
(Tables~\ref{tab:matrix_instr} and~\ref{tab:matrix_base}).
(1) \emph{One dial:} rewards order as single
$<$ SBoN$(.1)\!\approx\!$BoL$(.1)$ $<$ SBoN$(.02)\!\approx\!$BoL$(.02)$
$<$ BoP$(3)$ $<$ BoP$(15)\!\approx\!$BoN, and detection orders exactly in
reverse --- the head parameter positions the output on a single
reward--evidence trade-off, and the perturbation family (Gumbel vs.\
Laplace vs.\ Poissonized $n$) is immaterial at matched strength, as
Proposition~\ref{prop:tilt} predicts (the tilt is proportional to the
selection pressure on reward, with slope set by the within-pool
reward--signal anticorrelation).
(2) \emph{Head-wise closure:} the candidate-separated arms track the
unwatermarked arm head by head, not only at hard BoN.
(3) \emph{Flat lines:} the shared-key arms are constant across all heads
--- with $1.0/16$ unique candidates every selection head degenerates to
single --- while the base model amplifies everything else: twice the BoN
gain ($0.0301$ vs.\ $0.0145$), much stronger per-text evidence
($z\approx13$ vs.\ $8$ for the shared Gumbel key), and a larger selection
tilt ($-4.6$ vs.\ $-1.5$ z at hard BoN), consistent with the higher entropy
of pre-alignment text.

\subsection{Third scheme family: biased-logit (KGW)}\label{app:kgw}
Published KGW biases greenlist logits by $\delta$ and \emph{samples}; each
candidate consumes fresh randomness, so the pool does not collapse: $16/16$
unique candidates, and the normalized Best-of-$16$ gain (the BoN gain divided
by the within-prompt reward spread) is $1.62$ vs.\ $1.67$ unwatermarked ---
no measurable attenuation, consistent with $\rho_g\approx0$ in
Proposition~\ref{prop:moments}(iii). Its cost lies on the marginal axis, as
the scheme is not distortion-free: single-sample reward $0.0654$ vs.\
$0.0703$, median perplexity $5.67$ vs.\ $3.82$; Best-of-$16$ recovers most
but not all of the gap ($0.0837$ vs.\ $0.0848$) --- selection compensates
marginal distortion \citep{verma2025watermarking} but cannot remove it.

Derandomizing the sampler (keyed inverse-CDF sampling of the biased
distribution, deterministic given key and context --- the design point of
deterministic deployments such as SynthID-Text, and of any pipeline that
requires reproducible generation for caching or audit) produces exactly the
collapse of \S\ref{sec:collapse}; separating the \emph{sampling} stream
per candidate (greenlist untouched) restores it. Because the detector reads
only the greenlist, which remains keyed exactly as before, the detector is
unchanged: no union test and no threshold cost --- for this family the lift
is free on the detection side.

\subsection{Reasoning-side evaluation: GSM8K}\label{app:gsm}
We repeat the design on mathematical reasoning, where test-time scaling is
measured in accuracy rather than reward: GSM8K \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}, first
$128$ test problems, LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, raw $4$-shot prompts, $N{=}16$
candidates (temperature $1.0$, top-$p$ $0.95$, $288$ tokens, truncated at
the next exemplar boundary). Selection heads are reasoning-native and
key-independent: pass@$1$ (mean over candidates), pass@$16$ (any candidate
correct --- an oracle verifier), and self-consistency majority voting over
extracted final answers \citep{wang2023selfconsistency}. Detection uses
each arm's own detector with thresholds at the $0.01$ quantile of $2048$
unwatermarked GSM8K generations; for the candidate-separated arm we report
two verification tiers: \emph{index-known} (the provider retains the
selected candidate's index, so a single-key test suffices) and
\emph{index-blind} (union over the $16$ subkeys). The last column is a
\emph{key-less} observer: AUROC of candidate log-perplexity against
unwatermarked text ($0.5$ = invisible), the empirical face of the
distortion-freeness certificate.

Findings. (1) \emph{Collapse on the accuracy axis}: the shared key loses
$39.1$ points of pass@16 (paired CI $[-47.7,-30.5]$) and $27.3$ points of
self-consistency accuracy --- all sixteen votes are the same answer.
Candidate separation restores both to the unwatermarked level (all paired
differences contain zero); the maj@$k$ curve rises from $.53$ to $.80$ for
the unwatermarked, candidate-separated, and softened arms alike, and stays
flat at $.547$ for the shared key.
(2) \emph{The softened workaround taxes every sample}: its pass@1 is
$4.2$ points below unwatermarked (CI $[-7.2,-1.2]$), a significant
distortion cost that the reward axis of Table~\ref{tab:main} does not
resolve; pool-level metrics mask it at $N{=}16$. KGW's marginal distortion
costs $11.0$ points of pass@1, and majority voting does not fully compensate
($-7.0$ points at maj@16, CI $[-12.5,-1.6]$): voting cannot undo a bias that
afflicts every vote.
(3) \emph{Detection on short, low-entropy reasoning chains is weak for
every scheme} (median $z\approx3$ against $\approx8$ on the dialogue
domain): chain-of-thought text carries few high-entropy positions. At this
signal level the multiplicity fee of index-blind verification is material
(thresholds $2.71$ vs.\ $3.71$; TPR $.667\to.421$ on the released answer),
matching the $\ln N/z$ penalty of Theorem~\ref{thm:vanish}(iii); a
provider who logs the selected index --- available for free in any BoN
pipeline, and recoverable as the union detector's argmax --- pays none of
it. The selection tilt appears here too:
$\mathrm{corr}(\text{correct},z)\approx-0.22$, correct solutions being
more model-typical.
(4) \emph{The certificate, measured}: a key-less observer equipped with the
model separates strict watermarked text from unwatermarked at AUROC
$.487$/$.460$ (chance), while the key holder detects at $.667$; KGW leaks
nearly as much to the public ($.599$) as its own detector yields at the
$1\%$ operating point ($.555$).

\subsection{Calibration}
Union detector on unwatermarked text ($128$ prompts $\times16$ candidates):
realized FPR $0.055/0.012$ at nominal $0.05/0.01$ before Bonferroni tightening
of the selected-output evaluation ($0.039/0.008$, Table~\ref{tab:suite});
subkey-generated text scored under the \emph{base} key is null
($0.057$ at the $0.05$ level), confirming cryptographic separation of the
derived streams.
